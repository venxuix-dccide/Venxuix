<h2 align="center"><img src="../../assets/Ve%20White%20%28hud%29.png" width="24" align="left" alt="Ve"><img src="../../assets/Ve%20White%20%28hud%29.png" width="24" align="right" alt="Ve">Agent Xtension<br></h2>

<p align="center">A diagram is not a picture of one thing. Nothing about diagramming is fixed. It has no native subject, no chart it was born to draw, and the moment you sell it as the tool that diagrams this or that, you have made it smaller than it is. Agent Xtension treats it as what it actually is: a harmonic template, a way of holding sources beside each other until the relation between them is the thing you can see. What it draws is whatever you were already working on, whether that is a conversation, an architecture, a project, an assignment or a codebase. The lines carry as much as the nodes; the red, yellow, green, purple, & white wires connecting the sources are not decoration but routes, the different stories the same sources tell depending on the path you take through them.</p>

<p align="center">This is the surface a model draws on. Ask for a diagram today and the LLM answers in ASCII, the right instinct capped at the handful of characters a text box can hold. Agent Xtension is a graph engineering workspace, and it is where that topological answer goes instead: the model connects, writes what it is responding to, and the reply arrives as something the user can visually decode. It then saves as an artifact you can walk back into. Folders show you the source directories, but never how those directories communicate with each other; that harmonic relationship is the one thing a tree cannot draw. With the Agent Xtension, an LLM tasked to explain a codebase can understand the map before navigating into any file: The perception of users executions/intent stops being something you assemble in just text by pairing it with something you visually decode before specifications.</p>

<p align="center">Each topology above came from a different ask, and they are best read as one workflow rather than a catalogue. What they share is not their subject and not what is inside them; it is the single rule underneath all of them. The shape of the topology has to be harmonic to the way humans already decode shapes. Before you have read a word on a page your eyes have already taken in which way its lines run, what sits near what, and what is crowded against what is empty. The anatomy of the eye does that on its own, without being asked, and it takes the whole field at once. Written language is the late arrival, borrowing a part of the brain that spent far longer recognizing shapes and the junctions where lines meet. Shapes are not a substitute for the first language; they are the first language.</p>

<p align="center">Shapes arrive whole; a path does not. Connect those same shapes with lines that cross and double back and the diagram buries its sense of direction in its own wiring, because following a line end to end is slow work that gets slower with every crossing. Requiring the viewer to retrace vs understanding sense of direction at first sight. Most graph software optimizes the graph and leave the geometrical alignment to chance. Here the layouts are a batch of geometric templates, random to the asking but harmonic to the situation, so that what matters is carried where the eye is already looking: what leads is placed ahead, what belongs together is held together, what closes is drawn closed. Strip every title and name off these and the story still holds, told by geometry/wired connections/ and hue alone. That is a philosophy wired into the software, not a style applied over it.</p>
