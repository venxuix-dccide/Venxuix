<p align="center">World is where the workspace gets its atmosphere. Any environment you own can be cast around the canvas — not hung behind it, but resolved onto a curved geometry that acts as a firmament for your visual workspace. It holds the same whether you're working in 2D or 3D; moving through it only changes your vantage. Inside the firmament, nothing changes. Around it, everything can. <h1 align="center"><img src="../../assets/Ve%20White%20%28hud%29.png" width="24" align="left" alt="Ve"><img src="../../assets/Ve%20White%20%28hud%29.png" width="24" align="right" alt="Ve">Agency<br></h1>

<p align="center">An agency is not a menu of automations. It's a roster — people you place, watch, and hold to a day. Agency is where that roster becomes visible: you put agents on the thing you're working on and see them sitting there, you say when they're expected, you keep the room they work in, and you keep the record of what they did in it. Halo is where you seat them, Schedule is when they're due, Projects is what they return to, and Fiaona Live is what remains afterward. Every part answers one question from a different side — who is on this, and what came of it. Continue to <a href="#agency-halo">Halo</a> · <a href="#saved-workflow-templates">Projects</a> · <a href="#agency-fiaona-live">Fiaona</a>.</p>

<p align="center">A halo is your team, drawn as a ring around the work. In the shots below, V.E holds the core while Live Stream, Eval One, Eval Two and Dish sit out on the base around it — five assigned, and you can read that at a glance without opening anything, because the assignment <em>is</em> the picture. Click the core and the controls follow the core; click a layer and they follow the layer, up to twelve rings deep, so a crowded job stacks outward instead of collapsing into a list. Spin it, recolor it, drop an image into it, and the whole arrangement stays where the work is — flat on the ground when you're standing in the world, facing you when you're on the canvas. You never go somewhere else to see who's on something. You look at it. Continue to <a href="#saved-workflow-templates">Project Workflows</a>.</p>

<h3 align="center"><img src="../../assets/Ve%20White%20%28hud%29.png" width="24" align="left" alt="Ve"><img src="../../assets/Ve%20White%20%28hud%29.png" width="24" align="right" alt="Ve">Internal vs External Workspace<br></h3>

<table>
<tr><th align="center"><img src="../../assets/OO-126%289%29.gif" width="100%" alt="Internal vs External Workspace"></th></tr>
<tr><td align="center">
<p>Now the opposite architecture, the external one. Start a workspace from the Venxuix button in the top left and it opens as a fresh process on your hardware: not a second project inside the first, and not a canvas, which is the internal way. Every click on the Venxuix title starts an external VNX workspace that doesn't share internal relations. You can carry however many VNX workspaces to containerize however many Canvas workflows your hardware can handle. Two workspaces started from the Venxuix button, both alive, and neither knows the other exists; whatever you build in one never leans on the other, and if one ever goes down, the rest never feel it.</p>
<p>Alt Q is Venxuix’s own switcher, and it deliberately shows only Venxuix workspaces. The system's Alt Tab shows every window on your machine; Alt Q shows your homes. And because Venxuix holds softwares inside it, the two clean each other up: four VS Codes living inside a workspace would have been four entries in Alt Tab, but here they surface as one Venxuix. Two workspaces might hold fourteen applications between them, and you queue between two, not fourteen. The switchers run off the same held key, in harmony: Tab reaches the rest of your system, Q reaches your workspaces, and you can cross from one to the other without ever letting go.</p>
<p>Where you press Alt Q also decides where a workspace lands. The panel opens on the monitor your mouse is on, and the workspace you choose is summoned to that monitor: the window travels to you, not you to it. Alt Tab was never architected to do specifically that: it’s a recall surface, built to show everything running and return you to a window exactly where it last sat. It answers where you were; Alt Q answers bring it here. The two complete each other rather than compete. And the queue sees both architectures. Standalone preview shows the external side, one card for every Venxuix process alive on your machine. Canvas preview turns inward and shows the internal side, every canvas pooled from across all of your workspaces, and choosing one raises the workspace that holds it. <a href="../../assets/OO-126%289%29.mp4">Watch video</a></p>
</td></tr>
</table>
